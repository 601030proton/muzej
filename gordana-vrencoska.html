Gordana Vrencoska trained in painting and fashion design in Skopje and studied design at Central Saint Martins in London. Her work spans textiles, graphic design, painting, and installation.

Secondary Archive

Selected projects

Bridal Flag (2021) hangs square to the wall. Three dark-centered circles run across its middle, inside a red border. Vrencoska took the flag of the Galičnik Wedding as her starting point. In her account of the work, the old flag marked a bride’s entry into a household in which she was expected to give up part of her own identity. The redesign keeps the flag as a public object and gives it a different subject: the bride herself.

From a notebook to an exhibition

Vrencoska’s mother kept a notebook containing drawings of herself and her daughters. Those pages survived alongside other childhood work that Vrencoska showed in 2006, in an exhibition about how a person becomes an artist. A decade later, UNEXPOSED: My Side Stories brought other working material into view, including the exhibition catalogs and brochures she had collected. They record part of an artist’s education that rarely appears on a résumé: what she went to see and what she thought worth bringing home.

At the threshold

For Soft Barrier (2002), Vrencoska hung PVC curtains across eight doorways at Skopje’s Kale fortress. In one photograph, a yellow curtain fills the arch of a rough stone opening. The installation used the existing entrances as its supports.

For Equestrian Lane (2014), Vrencoska proposed a riding-lane sign beside the Warrior on Horse monument in Skopje’s central square, extending the presence of a horse on a pedestal into the city’s traffic system. The proposal was never realized. In London, she used an existing space directly, staging The Queen of Turbine Hall (2008) in Tate Modern’s vast former engine room.
