Borka Avramova (1924–1993) was born in Tetovo. She studied at the art school in Skopje and at the Academy of Fine Arts in Zagreb under Frano Kršinić. She returned to Macedonia in 1953 and moved to Zagreb in 1960.

Secondary Archive

Selected projects

In Head of a Girl (Romani Woman) (1953), the line from the forehead to the lips is sharply defined against the light. The hair, drawn back from the face, forms a single rounded mass. Avramova used plaster for the portrait now in the National Gallery of Macedonia. In the similarly sized Young Highlander 2, the edge of the cap and the broader planes of the face give the head a different weight.

Eleven sculptures in Skopje

In October 1954, Avramova presented eleven sculptures at the Cultural Center of Construction Workers near Freedom Square. The Museum of Contemporary Art’s 2012 retrospective catalogue describes it as Macedonia’s first solo sculpture exhibition and its first solo exhibition by a woman artist. Reviewing the show, Dimče Koco singled out the people she chose to portray, whom he associated with markets, villages, and daily work. He also noted the broad handling of the surfaces. In the early portraits, a change in a cheek or a brow can carry much of the likeness.

From the portrait to the square

Scale became a different problem in Tetovo. After a public competition, Avramova made Victory (1961), also known as the Monument to the Revolution. A standing bronze figure occupies one part of the site. A separate wall holds a bronze relief of scenes from the wartime Partisan struggle. Two years later, Shield 1 compressed relief into an object just 56 centimeters high. The faces on the bronze share a single curved surface.
