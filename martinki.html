Traditions

Martinki

Words Aleksandar Trajkovski

Red and white wool, the first swallow, and a song for the beginning of spring.

On the first day of March in Kukuš, children carried a wooden swallow from house to house. They sang about a bird crossing the Black Sea. The Miladinov brothers recorded the opening line in their 1861 collection, alongside a description of the red and white yarn that adults tied around children’s necks, wrists, and ankles for health. In the same entry, they note that people in Struga wore the threads to greet the swallows.

Ластовица идеше, црно море пливаше…

A swallow was coming, swimming across the Black Sea…

Twisting the thread

A martinka (мартинка) is a small charm made by twisting two strands together, commonly red and white wool. The Institute for Folklore “Marko Cepenkov” records that people made them the night before the first of March or, in some places, at sunrise. The day goes by several names, including Marta and Letnik. Where families kept the old calendar, its first of March fell on March 14 in the civil calendar.

The thread was worn for health and good luck in the months ahead. Some people also understood it as protection from snakes. The colors varied. Macedonia’s heritage inventory records the use of dark wool in some places, alongside the familiar red and white.

The first swallow

In the Macedonian accounts, seeing the first migratory bird could signal that it was time to take the thread off. Some people hung it from a tree. Others left it under a stone. In the wider regional custom, a flowering tree or a stork could play the same part. UNESCO inscribed these March practices in 2017 in a joint entry for Macedonia, Bulgaria, Moldova, and Romania.

The Miladinovs’ entry preserves two ways of noticing the same change in season. In Struga, people wore the thread as the swallows returned. In Kukuš, children made a bird they could carry to the next door.

Sources

Miladinov, Dimitar and Konstantin. “Customs,” in the Miladinov Brothers’ Collection. Zagreb, 1861. The Kukuš entry records the song fragment and mentions Struga. English translation by Muzej.

Institute for Folklore “Marko Cepenkov” and the Cultural Heritage Protection Office. “The Custom Martinki.” Macedonian heritage inventory and protection decision.

UNESCO. “Cultural Practices Associated to the 1st of March.” Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, 2017.
