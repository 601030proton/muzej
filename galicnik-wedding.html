Galičnik Wedding

Words Aleksandar Trajkovski

The Galičnik Wedding, or Galička svadba, is a public festival built around the marriage rites of a Mijak village on Mount Bistra in western Macedonia.

St. Peter’s Day and the wedding rites

In the 1920s, Czech painter and collector Ludvík Kuba came to Galičnik during a week when several families were holding weddings. The timing followed the calendar of men who earned their living away from the village. They came home for Petrovden, St. Peter’s Day, observed locally on July 12. For families whose lives were organized around pečalba, or work away from home, the feast gave them a week when people could gather for a marriage.

Olive Lodge, who observed the wedding customs in the 1920s, describes the bride’s party setting out for water by torchlight. Kuba understood the trip as one of the bride’s last duties to her parents’ household. The groom, meanwhile, rode out to invite his future in-laws. The bride later rode to her future father-in-law’s house. The marriage ceremony came on another day, after the two households had already made a series of visits and exchanges.

Bridal dress

The British Museum’s bridal coat from Galičnik has twenty pairs of filigree buttons along the front. The cream wool coat, called a klasenik guvealski, dates to about 1900–1920. It reaches the knee and has short vestigial sleeves. Along the opening, metal-wrapped thread catches the light against deep red cloth. The embroidery above the braided cord forms small branching twigs.

The public festival

The festival still takes place on a weekend around Petrovden. Researcher Iliya Nedin dates the public festival to 1963. The village council gives 1991 as the first edition of the modern Galičnik Wedding.

Photographs from two consecutive editions show how the program could change. In 1993, Julija Petrevska and Vladimir Damčevski married at the festival. A photographer who documented both years records that the 1994 edition presented the customs without a marriage ceremony.

In 2026, the village council chose Martina Mihajlova and Aleksandar Pendaroski from a pool of applicants. Pendaroski’s parents had married at the 1991 edition. The program began with the raising of the wedding flag and led to the couple’s marriage in the church of Saints Peter and Paul.
