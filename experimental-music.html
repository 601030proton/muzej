An Independent Frequency

Ten recordings from Macedonia’s experimental music scene, and the rooms, labels, and radio station that helped carry them.

In the summer of 2026, the independent music site Mono-Ton booked Chlen 16 for its first birthday. The lineup was bass, drums, and synthesizer, with two of the three musicians on vocals. Studio Alshar in Skopje appears in the credits for their July single, “Нејќам веќе ваков мир.” It also appears on a Sharla release whose principal instrument is the accordion. Read the sleeves and the connections start to show up in places a genre label can’t explain.

Chlen 16 · Mrtov Konj

Нејќам веќе ваков мир

The title means “I don’t want this kind of peace anymore.” The words bring the phrase down to the scale of a life spent waiting for a job. Luka Jankulovski plays synth and sings, Marko Pečenović plays bass and sings, and Blagojče Aceski plays drums.

A frequency becomes a label

Kanal 103 began broadcasting from Skopje in 1991. Listeners call it Stotrojka, after the number on the dial. The station moved online after losing its FM frequency in 2019. At its thirtieth anniversary in 2021, it stopped the stream for a day in protest. The name now appears on records as well as broadcasts. In the case of Fydhws, it ties a film and its soundtrack shaped by walks in Prague to a label run by the Skopje station.

Fydhws · Stotrojka

oд почеток?

Kristijan Petreski built a short film from footage gathered around Prague, then wrote music in response to the scenes. His notes trace the first musical ideas to a shot filmed in Stromovka Park.

Toni Dimitrov · Kirigirisu Recordings

Ghosts of Atlantis

Two pieces, both long enough to let your ears adjust to their small differences. Try the title piece at a low volume. Start with the sound that holds steady, then listen for what passes across it.

Lazar Darkovski · Elektramusic

Egon

Darkovski came to this music through percussion and visual art. Begin with “Bel um” and notice how much time he gives each piano figure to disappear before the next one takes its place.

The names in the credits

Gola Planina put out a compilation of the Macedonian new wave from 2022. Open it and Vagina Corporation’s “Германски рок” is track one. The collective started the Mrtov Konj label in 2024. It also took its festival, Sepak Se Vrti, on the road. In an interview with Sloboden Pečat, Martin Džorlev names the local organizers who helped make that possible, among them Dona in Gostivar, Boiling Point in Kumanovo, and Goongula in Prilep.

The credits on the Lea Carine record extend the map again. Rade Bikovski, who recorded Chlen 16’s single, mixed and mastered the album. Gjorgji Janevski contributed field recordings. Two sets of names on two releases give you a glimpse of the people working between bands.

Lea Carine · Mrtov Konj

Lea Carine

Andrej Dimovski and Aleksandra Arsovska fit eight tracks into about 24 minutes. The record uses speech and field recordings alongside guitar and drums. Listen for the moment a voice stops carrying the song and begins to sound like a conversation you’ve walked in on.

Open on Bandcamp

Pondskull · Mrtov Konj

Pondskull

There are two tracks here. The second runs just over an hour and documents the Pondskull Interdisciplinary Musical Ensemble in front of an audience at Skopje’s Social Center Dunja on January 23, 2026. Play it as a set and give it the time you’d give the room.

Open on Bandcamp

Alembic · Self-released

Без никого да удриш

Iva Damjanovski’s credits list voice, synthesizer and theremin. Dragan Teodosiev plays drums on this second studio album. Try “Анемоја,” then go back to the beginning and hear how often a song changes its scale.

What the instruments can do

There is another route through these records. Follow Filip Bukrshliev’s guitar from the Sveti Nikole Session to Svirci Vardarci. One record gives him a clarinet to answer. On the other, listen to what happens when the accordion can hold a note after the guitar has let it go. Then put on Rodzevski and listen to how much a voice can carry before anybody else has to enter.

Blagojche Tomevski, Filip Bukrshliev & Dorjan Jovanovikj · PMG Jazz

Sveti Nikole Session Vol. 1

The first volume is a single 37-minute piece, recorded at the Krste Misirkov cultural center in Sveti Nikole. Tomevski plays clarinets and flutes, Bukrshliev plays guitar, and Jovanovikj plays Chapman Stick and electronics. The later volumes change the lineup.

Open on Bandcamp

Svirci Vardarci · Sharla

Svirci Vardarci LP

Yordan Kostov’s accordion is the instrument to follow. On “Giska mijaca,” he plays with electric guitarist Filip Bukrshliev and drummer Tejka Vasiljevic. The credits for the opener, “Na vrba,” list a different trio.

Open on Bandcamp

Kristo Rodzevski · Port of Entry

Orthodoxia Kai Thanatos

The Bitola-born singer gives Orthodox chant the room to set its own pace. Listen to “Coptic Hymn,” then to “Meteora,” where Thomas Fehlmann is credited. The two tracks are a useful way into a record that places the solitary voice alongside electronic collaborations.
