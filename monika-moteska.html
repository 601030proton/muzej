Monika Moteska was born in Prilep in 1971. She graduated in painting from the Faculty of Fine Arts in Skopje in 1996 and works in painting, photography, video, and installation.

Artist’s website

Selected Works

Metal needles pass through white marble in Embroidery in Stone (2020), an installation shown at Čifte Amam in Skopje. The needles are larger than the ones used to sew cloth. Moteska describes the effort of pushing a needle through stone, where it would ordinarily pass through silk or linen, as a metaphor for the difficulty of living.

Projection and performance

At a former factory in Vienna, Fire Eater (1998) set three projected images of Moteska’s face above flames in metal vessels. Sand covered the floor and made the audience’s footsteps part of the installation. The same year, in Achilles’ Heel, she projected moving legs onto a performer’s torso in a former maternity ward at CIX Gallery in Skopje. In Challenger (1999), the knives around a projected figure were small sculptures she made herself.

In Insect Taxidermy (2016), Moteska uses pencil and hair in a series of drawings. She describes following the insects from flight to the moment they stuck to paper and died.

Markovi Kuli

In Breathing Beneath (2018), Moteska filled a bathtub with earth and shaped it into a small mountain. Behind it she projected footage made at Markovi Kuli, the rocky heights above her hometown of Prilep. She returned to the site for Helmet Choir (2020). For that video, she covered military helmets with human hair, leaving their holes and dents visible, then sent them rolling down the rocks. The noise of the helmets striking one another is part of the work.

Downhill (2019) places a rock beside a drawing of a rocky landscape. In Salvation According to Beuys (2020), an enlarged metal sled holds the framed landscape above the floor, with a climbing rope incorporated into the installation.

Landscape Experience

Moteska and Robert Jankuloski presented Landscape Experience at the 59th Venice Biennale in 2022. The installation used moving images, photographs, and objects to consider what war and the exploitation of land leave behind. In one view of the work, a pile of round objects sits in front of a projected scene, taking up part of the same floor the visitors stand on. The National Gallery brought the project to Čifte Amam in Skopje the following year.
